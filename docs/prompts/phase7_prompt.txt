## 8. MASTER VOICE IDENTITY AND SPEAKER VERIFICATION

Implement a secure voice identity enrollment system.

Each user may enroll their voice through a guided verification process.

The enrollment workflow should:

1. Authenticate the account.
2. Explain how voice verification works.
3. Obtain explicit consent.
4. Capture multiple spoken samples.
5. Generate a protected speaker-verification template.
6. Store the template securely.
7. Associate it with the authenticated account.
8. Allow re-enrollment and revocation.

### Voice verification

When the user issues a protected command, evaluate whether the speaker matches the enrolled profile.

Implement:

- Speaker verification.
- Anti-replay protections.
- Liveness checks where technically supported.
- Confidence thresholds.
- Repeated-attempt limits.
- Voice enrollment revocation.
- Verification audit records.

A voice match must not be treated as infallible authentication.

Recorded speech, voice cloning, background speakers and deepfakes can defeat poorly designed voice systems.

### Multi-factor protection

For sensitive operations, require additional authentication, such as:

- Account password.
- Passkey.
- Device authentication.
- Explicit user confirmation.

Voice verification may provide convenience, but must not be the sole security factor for high-impact operations.

Provide a secure fallback when voice verification fails.

Do not permanently lock a legitimate user out because of microphone quality or speech changes.
